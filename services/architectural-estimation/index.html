Quantities that match the sheets

Architectural estimation here means measuring the drawings: bills of quantities, take-offs, material schedules and the bundles contractors send out for pricing. The numbers are taken from the plans, elevations and schedules, and the assumptions are written down. A quantity that cannot be seen on a drawing is marked as an assumption, not hidden inside a total.

Stone quantities and slab layouts can sit inside a marble package or be measured on their own. Joinery, flooring, wall finishes and ceiling areas are measured when those drawings exist. The work is for contractors and estimators in Dubai, Abu Dhabi, Sharjah and the other emirates. It is quoted per package. There is no published rate per page or per square metre.

Deliverables and formats

  • A bill of quantities organised so a revision of the drawings can be compared with the previous issue.
  • Take-off notes that say which sheet each figure came from.
  • Material schedules for stone, joinery or finishes, ready to send to a supplier.
  • Spreadsheet files, plus PDF when you need a frozen copy for a tender.
  • An updated issue when the design or the site size changes, quoted as its own step if the change is large.

DWG and PDF remain the drawing formats when drawings are part of the same quote. The spreadsheet is the format for the numbers. If you only need a cutting list for one bathroom, that is a smaller job than a tender bill for a whole villa. Say which one you want.

Using the bill in a UAE fit-out

On projects in Dubai, Abu Dhabi and Sharjah the bill is often attached to the same submission as the shop drawings. The consultant comments on the drawings; the contractor uses the bill to price or to order. When a comment changes a height or a stone thickness, the quantities have to move with the revision or the order will not match the approved sheet. D3DDD updates the figures against the revised issue when that update is in the quote. Nothing here is a claim about a particular tender law or a mandatory bill format. If your client has a template, send it and the schedules will follow the template.

Measured stone work you can compare with a schedule includes bathroom slab nesting and bathroom marble cladding. The nesting note on the slab nesting and cutting lists is the practical side of a cutting list.

Turnaround and the written quote

Every drawing service on this site is quoted per project. The delivery date is part of that quote and is confirmed before work starts. A comment round is a later issue, scheduled when the comments arrive. There is no published day-rate and no published square-metre rate. How to read the schedule

A take-off is only useful if someone else can check it. Each line names the sheet it came from and the unit: square metres of flooring, linear metres of skirting, or a count of sink cut-outs. When a villa has both a stone package and a joinery package, the two schedules stay separate so a stone yard is not asked to price cabinet carcasses. If the consultant already uses an empty bill template, send it with the drawings and the numbers will follow that column order. Assumptions such as joint width or cutting waste sit in the header of the schedule, not buried in a cell, so the person pricing the work can change them.

Quantity take-off and the bill of quantities

A quantity take-off is the measurement itself: areas, linear metres and counts taken from the plans, elevations and schedules, with a note of which sheet each figure came from. A bill of quantities (BOQ) is the structured document those measurements are organised into, so a revision of the drawings can be compared with the previous issue and a contractor or supplier can price or order from it. Both already sit on this service. The live page also offers material schedules and, when asked, an early cost plan to compare options before tender.

D3DDD does not publish a mandatory UAE bill format or a tender-law claim. If the client already has an empty template, send it with the drawings and the schedules follow that column order — that rule is already on the live page. Assumptions such as joint width or cutting waste stay in the header, not hidden in a total. The blog explains what a quantity take-off is and how a BOQ differs from an estimate and a quotation.

What can be measured

Stone quantities (areas, linear metres, piece counts, with the cutting-waste assumption written down) can sit inside a marble shop drawings package or be measured from drawings you already have. A stone-specific how-to is the blog guide How to calculate marble quantities and reduce waste. This service page is wider than that article: joinery, flooring, wall finishes and ceiling areas are measured when those drawings exist, and the stone schedule is kept separate from a joinery schedule so a yard is not asked to price cabinet carcasses.

The numbers only match the build if they move with the drawing revision. When a consultant comment changes a height or a thickness, the take-off has to be updated against that issue. Large redesigns are quoted as their own step, as the live FAQ already states.

Estimation is quoted per package. It is not a published square-metre or per-page rate, and it is not a website package price. Send the drawings (and a template, if you have one) through the contact form. If you also need the sheets measured from, see architectural drafting.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What is an estimation package?

A bill of quantities, take-off, material schedule or tender bundle measured from your drawings, with the assumptions written down. Delivered as a spreadsheet and PDF.

Are drawing fees included?

Only when drawings are part of the same quote. A numbers-only package does not include new DWG sheets.

Do quantities update after consultant comments?

Yes, when the update is included. A large redesign is quoted as its own step so the bill stays tied to the approved revision.

Can you measure stone only?

Yes. Slab areas, piece counts and cutting waste can be scheduled with the marble drawings or from drawings you already have.

Is there a fixed rate?

No. Estimation is quoted per package. It is not a published square-metre or per-page rate, and it is not a website package price.

What is a quantity take-off, and how is it different from a BOQ?

A take-off is the measurement from the drawings. A BOQ is the structured bill those measurements are placed in, organised so a later revision can be compared and a contractor or supplier can price or order from it. This service prepares both, plus material schedules when they are in the quote.

Which trades and units can you measure?

Whatever is visible on the drawings in the scope: for example square metres of flooring or finishes, linear metres of skirting, and counts of cut-outs. Stone, joinery, wall finishes and ceilings are measured when those sheets exist. A quantity that cannot be seen on a drawing is marked as an assumption.

Do you follow a standard UAE tender template?

Only if you send one. There is no single format claimed on this page. If your client has a template, the schedules follow it.

Can you prepare a take-off from PDFs only?

Yes, when the figures can be read from the PDF set. Say so in the enquiry. New DWG sheets are included only when drawings are part of the same quote.
