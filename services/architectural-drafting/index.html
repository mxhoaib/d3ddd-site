What architectural drafting covers here

Architectural drafting at D3DDD is the measured AutoCAD work that follows a design: shop drawings, fit-out sheets, joinery elevations, as-built records and, when you ask for it, a permit-oriented set prepared from your design package. Requirements for permits differ by emirate and by the authority that project actually uses, so the checklist has to come from you. The studio does not publish a code clause or a submission portal as if it were universal.

The usual client is a fit-out contractor, a joinery workshop or a consultant who needs the sheets redrawn so a site team can build from them. Shoaib Hussan draws them in Dubai for projects across the UAE. Source material can be a consultant’s PDF, an older DWG, a marked-up print or a survey. Say which layers and which title block the project already uses; matching them saves a comment round.

Deliverables and formats

  • DWG and PDF of the same issue, with a revision table.
  • Plans, elevations, sections, reflected ceiling plans and the details the scope names.
  • Joinery elevations with heights, materials and hardware notes when joinery is included.
  • As-built sheets that record what was built, for handover or a later alteration.
  • English notes, with Arabic on request.

DWG is the file other consultants coordinate against. PDF is the file that gets marked up. A stone package that needs cutting lists is better handled on the marble page, and the two can be quoted together if the job has both. Layer habits for Gulf fit-out packages are discussed in AutoCAD standards for Gulf fit-out.

Review in Dubai, Abu Dhabi and Sharjah

Drafted fit-out sets in these emirates normally travel with the contractor’s submission to the project consultant. The architect or interior designer checks them against the approved design: partitions, ceiling heights, joinery lines, wet-area build-ups and the points where trades meet. Comments come back on the PDF or as a list. The next issue raises the revision and clouds the changes so the site does not build from a mixed set. Abu Dhabi jobs often ask for a transmittal; Sharjah jobs sometimes have only the designer as reviewer; Dubai jobs range from a single interior designer to a full consultant team. The drawing follows the rule you send. It is not, by itself, a permit or a BIM submission. If the project also needs those, they are a separate line in the quote. Background reading is on drawing-package notes for Dubai and Dubai BIM submission notes.

External and wet-area sheets in the portfolio include pool and outdoor majlis tiling. Floor set-out that starts as a drafting plan includes villa ground-floor flooring layout.

Turnaround and the written quote

Every drawing service on this site is quoted per project. The delivery date is part of that quote and is confirmed before work starts. A comment round is a later issue, scheduled when the comments arrive. There is no published day-rate and no published square-metre rate. FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What is included in architectural drafting?

Shop drawings, fit-out and joinery sheets, CAD details and as-built records, issued as DWG and PDF. Permit-oriented sheets are prepared only from the checklist you supply, because requirements differ by emirate and by project.

How are comments handled?

Comments on the PDF are revised into a new issue with a raised revision. The delivery date for that issue is agreed when the comments arrive. The first date is the one written into the quote.

Do you follow a Gulf layer standard?

The set can follow the layer names and title block you already use. Send a sample. A practical note on fit-out standards is on the blog; it is guidance, not a claim that one standard is mandatory.

Can stone and joinery be in one quote?

Yes. Say so in the enquiry. Marble shop drawings have their own page because the cutting list and slab layout are a distinct deliverable.

Which emirates?

Dubai, Abu Dhabi, Sharjah, Ajman, Ras Al Khaimah, Umm Al Quwain and Fujairah. Files are sent digitally.
