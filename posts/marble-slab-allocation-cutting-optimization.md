**Slab allocation decides which marble piece is cut from which slab. Cutting optimization arranges those pieces on each slab so the vein looks right and as little stone as possible is wasted.** Both are planned on drawings before the saw touches the stone.

## What is slab allocation?

Natural marble varies from slab to slab in colour, vein and size. Allocation assigns every piece on the cutting list to a specific slab, so that neighbouring pieces in the finished job look like they belong together.

## How is it done, step by step?

1. **Get the slab sizes.** Record the actual length, width and thickness of each slab, ideally from photos and measurements of the slabs in stock.
2. **Number the pieces.** Every floor, wall, vanity or step piece needs a unique number from the fabrication drawings.
3. **Group by area.** Keep pieces for one wall, one floor zone or one vanity on the same or consecutive slabs.
4. **Plan the vein direction.** Decide whether veins run along the length, across, or are mirrored (book matching) for feature walls and medallions.
5. **Place pieces on each slab.** Fit pieces within the slab outline, leaving room for the saw kerf and avoiding cracks, fissures or marked defects.
6. **Check and revise.** Confirm every piece is placed once, then update the cutting list to match.

## What is vein matching and book matching?

Vein matching aligns the pattern across a joint so it continues from one piece to the next. In book matching, two consecutive slabs are opened like a book so the veins mirror each other. These need slabs kept in sequence, which is why allocation is drawn before cutting, not after.

## How does cutting optimization reduce waste?

Fitting pieces tightly on a slab leaves fewer offcuts. Small pieces such as skirting and thresholds can use offcuts from larger ones. The trade-off is appearance: the tightest layout is not always the best-looking, so visible areas come first and hidden or small pieces fill the gaps.

## Common mistakes

1. Allocating before the slabs are physically measured.
2. Mixing slabs from different blocks in one visible area.
3. Not accounting for the saw kerf between pieces.
4. Changing the cutting list without updating the slab layout.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the difference between slab allocation and cutting optimization?
Allocation assigns pieces to slabs. Optimization arranges the pieces within each slab to cut waste.

### Do I need slab layouts for every project?
They matter most for visible natural stone with strong veining: feature walls, bathroom cladding, vanities and staircases.

### Can slab layouts be prepared remotely?
Yes, when you can share the slab photos or sizes and the piece drawings as digital files.
