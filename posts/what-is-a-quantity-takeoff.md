A quantity take-off is the measurement of a project from its drawings: areas, linear metres and counts, each tied back to the sheet it came from. It is not, by itself, a priced bid, and it is not the same document as a bill of quantities (BOQ), though the two are often prepared together. On fit-out and construction work in Dubai and across the UAE, a take-off is what a contractor uses to price, order or check that an approved revision has not changed the quantities they already sent out.

## What a quantity take-off is

A take-off starts from the drawings you actually have: plans, elevations, sections and schedules. Each line in the take-off names the sheet it came from and the unit being measured. Typical units, as already described on the estimation service page, include square metres of flooring or finishes, linear metres of skirting, and counts of items such as sink cut-outs.

Two rules make a take-off usable by someone who did not produce it:

- A quantity that cannot be seen on a drawing is marked as an assumption, not hidden inside a total.
- Assumptions that affect many lines — joint width, or cutting waste on stone — sit in the header of the schedule, not buried in a cell, so the person pricing the work can change them.

If those two rules are skipped, a later revision or a different wastage allowance can quietly make the order wrong, and no one can see why.

Piece or finish IDs are matched to the drawings so procurement and fabrication are talking about the same items. New DWG sheets are included only when drawings are part of the same quote. A numbers-only package does not invent a drawing set.

## How a take-off differs from a BOQ

The two terms are often used as if they were interchangeable. They are not.

A **quantity take-off** is the measurement itself: the working-out. It is the list of areas, lengths and counts, with sources and assumptions.

A **bill of quantities (BOQ)** is the structured document those measurements are organised into, so a revision of the drawings can be compared with the previous issue and a contractor or supplier can price or order from it. On the [estimation service](https://d3ddd.com/services/architectural-estimation/) this is already a listed deliverable: a BOQ organised so a later issue of the drawings can be compared with the earlier one.

You can have a take-off without issuing a formal bill, and you can have a bill that is little more than a re-typed take-off. In practice, UAE contractors often need both: the take-off as the checkable working, and the BOQ as the document that travels with a tender or an order. A longer explanation of how a BOQ sits next to an estimate and a quotation is a separate topic; the short version is that a take-off and a BOQ are measured documents, while a quotation is a priced offer.

D3DDD does not claim a single mandatory UAE bill format or a tender-law rule. If the client already has an empty template, send it with the drawings and the schedules follow that column order.

## How UAE contractors typically use a take-off

On projects in Dubai, Abu Dhabi and Sharjah the bill is often attached to the same submission as the shop drawings. The consultant comments on the drawings; the contractor uses the bill to price or to order. When a comment changes a height or a stone thickness, the quantities have to move with the revision or the order will not match the approved sheet. That sequence is already described on the estimation page. It is a description of how the documents travel, not a claim about a particular tender law.

A take-off is also useful before anyone tenders: as a check that a design option is even orderable, or as the basis for an early cost plan to compare finishes. Early cost framing to compare options before tender is a listed sub-service on the same page. It is still based on measurements from drawings, with assumptions written down.

If the design is still only a concept sketch, the figures will be rougher, and they should be labelled that way. A take-off from an approved, dimensioned set is a different job from a first pass on a presentation plan.
