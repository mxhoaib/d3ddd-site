For several years, **The Line** was the internet’s favourite architecture gif: a mirrored wall 170 kilometres long, 200 metres wide and 500 metres high, promising a car-free city for up to **nine million** people inside a 34 km² footprint. By 2026 the story has changed. Reporting through mid-2026 describes a **temporary work stoppage until at least 2030**, a sharp cut in near-term population ambitions for the wider NEOM zone, and a pivot toward **Oxagon**, the industrial and port district on the Red Sea ([The Architect’s Newspaper / Semafor coverage](https://www.archpaper.com/2026/06/neom-temporary-work-pause-the-line/); [BrentDesk synthesis](https://brentdesk.com/construction/neom-the-line-paused-until-after-2030-oxagon-priority); [ArchDaily overview](https://www.archdaily.com/1039911/the-line-at-a-crossroads-revisiting-neoms-vision-for-a-utopian-city)).

That does not make the design conversation worthless. It makes it more useful — if you separate **vision decks** from **buildable packages**.

## What was promised

NEOM presented The Line as a critique of sprawl: stack living, work and nature into a continuous linear structure instead of a road-dominated grid. Design language around “zero gravity urbanism,” layered ground planes and five-minute access to daily needs was showcased widely, including at the Venice Architecture Biennale context described in design press ([ArchDaily](https://www.archdaily.com/1039911/the-line-at-a-crossroads-revisiting-neoms-vision-for-a-utopian-city)).

Phase-one delivery partners named in industry releases included **Delugan Meissl Associated Architects (DMAA)** on urban design / masterplan work, **Gensler** on city planning coordination, and **Mott MacDonald** as city infrastructure engineer for vertical and horizontal systems ([Mott MacDonald](https://www.mottmac.com/en-middle-east/news/supporting-delivery-of-the-line-as-city-infrastructure-engineer/)). Those appointments signal how complex the real documentation tree would have been: not one hero façade drawing, but thousands of coordinated packages for structure, utilities, logistics tunnels and interiors.

## What actually got built — and paused

By late 2025 into 2026, public reporting described roughly **2.4 kilometres** of foundation / early works out of the planned **170 km**, with construction on The Line suspended and further work not expected before **2030** ([ArchDaily](https://www.archdaily.com/1039911/the-line-at-a-crossroads-revisiting-neoms-vision-for-a-utopian-city); [BrentDesk](https://brentdesk.com/construction/neom-the-line-paused-until-after-2030-oxagon-priority)). Population targets for the wider NEOM zone were revised downward several times from the original multi-million headline figures; recent coverage cites a maximum around **100,000** people for the zone by the end of the decade in some accounts, after earlier cuts from 1.5 million and 300,000 bands ([BrentDesk](https://brentdesk.com/construction/neom-the-line-paused-until-after-2030-oxagon-priority); [WIRED Middle East](https://www.wired.me/story/saudi-arabias-giga-projects-are-entering-their-reality-check)).

Saudi Arabia’s Public Investment Fund disclosed large write-downs on megaproject investments in 2025 reporting, with NEOM frequently named as a major contributor in secondary coverage ([BrentDesk](https://brentdesk.com/construction/neom-the-line-paused-until-after-2030-oxagon-priority)). Official messaging has tended to speak of “strategic discipline” and phasing rather than outright cancellation — a distinction that matters for contracts, but less for the viral render that sold the idea.

## Why Oxagon is the near-term story

Coverage of the pause consistently pairs it with a priority shift to **Oxagon**, NEOM’s industrial / logistics / port concept on the Red Sea, including floating and manufacturing ambitions and reported capital pledges in the billions ([The Architect’s Newspaper](https://www.archpaper.com/2026/06/neom-temporary-work-pause-the-line/)). Bjarke Ingels Group has been widely associated with Oxagon masterplanning in earlier announcements. For drafting and engineering firms, that pivot is the practical signal: **revenue-generating industrial and port packages** are easier to stage than a 500-metre mirrored city spanning a desert corridor.

WIRED Middle East framed the wider Vision 2030 giga-project moment as a “reality check” — costs, timelines and investor appetite forcing smaller, phased scopes ([WIRED Middle East](https://www.wired.me/story/saudi-arabias-giga-projects-are-entering-their-reality-check)). The Line is the loudest example, not the only one.

## Drafting lessons that survive the pause

Whether or not The Line resumes after 2030 in anything like its 2021 form, teams that prepared packages for NEOM-scale work already learned (or re-learned) several truths:

### 1. Renders are not shop drawings
A mirrored façade animation can win a keynote. It cannot tell a fabricator the gasket size, panel module, or maintenance cradle clearance. Clients who approve only the visual often discover clash problems when [AutoCAD drafting](/services/architectural-drafting/) and fabrication sets begin. Soften the gap early with coordinated plans, sections and [3D visualisation](/services/3d-rendering-modeling-visualization/) that match the same dimensions.

### 2. Phasing is a drawing deliverable
When a 170 km idea collapses to a few kilometres of foundations, the surviving value is in **phase packages**: what is buildable now, what is deferred, what utilities must still land. Every contractor website and every BOQ should expose that phasing clearly — which is why D3DDD’s [contractor website](/website-design.html) work stresses clear scopes, not just mood photography.

### 3. Linear cities still need piece-level stone and cladding logic
Even a shortened module needs wall elevations, joint layouts and cutting lists. [Marble and stone shop drawings](/marble.html) do not care whether the building is a mirrored megastructure or a Riyadh lobby; they care that piece W-14 matches the approved elevation.

### 4. Infrastructure engineers own the “boring” sheets that make cities work
Mott MacDonald’s role description — vertical/horizontal structures and city utility systems — is a reminder that the hero architecture sits on a mountain of civil and MEP documentation ([Mott MacDonald](https://www.mottmac.com/en-middle-east/news/supporting-delivery-of-the-line-as-city-infrastructure-engineer/)). Drafters who ignore risers, trenches and logistics tunnels will redraw forever.

### 5. Cost reality eventually edits the brief
Press estimates of The Line’s budget ranging into the trillions of dollars in early speculative reporting (as summarised by WIRED Middle East) show how quickly ambition outruns finance ([WIRED Middle East](https://www.wired.me/story/saudi-arabias-giga-projects-are-entering-their-reality-check)). Estimation and quantity take-off are not anti-design; they are how design stays buildable. See D3DDD [services](/services/) for how we pair drawings with measurable scopes.

## How to brief your next “vision” project without burning the shop

If a client arrives with a cinematic concept, ask for:

1. A **phase-one boundary** in metres, not slogans.
2. A **locked grid** and level datum before marketing sections go public.
3. A list of **fabrication packages** (stone, metal, glass, joinery) with owners.
4. Survey control and tolerance notes suitable for shop drawings.
5. A revision protocol that survives staff turnover when the programme pauses.

Those five questions would have saved many Gulf and international teams from drawing sets that chased a moving render.

## The Line after the headline

The pause until 2030 is not a reason to mock vertical urbanism wholesale. It is a reason to treat megaproject communication with the same scepticism you apply to an unchecked dimension string. The mirrored wall may return in a thinner, shorter or entirely different form. The drafting habits worth keeping are already clear: **coordinate early, phase honestly, and never confuse a video with a cutting list.**


## A short glossary for teams watching NEOM news

- **The Line:** the linear city concept inside NEOM; originally marketed at 170 km × 200 m × 500 m.
- **NEOM:** the broader development region / company vehicle in northwest Saudi Arabia.
- **Oxagon:** industrial, manufacturing and port-oriented district now prioritised in 2026 reporting.
- **Phase one:** whatever shortened module remains after strategic review — treat every “phase one” claim as a request for a drawn red-line boundary.
- **Write-down:** accounting recognition that prior investment value has fallen; not automatically the same as a cancelled building permit, but a hard signal to re-scope drawings.

Keeping this vocabulary straight stops a junior drafter from labelling a logistics shed sheet as “The Line façade package” because the folder name still says NEOM.

## Soft next steps for studios and fabricators

If your pipeline still includes Saudi giga-project conversations:

1. Re-open the latest **issued** set, not the 2022 marketing PDF.
2. Mark every sheet that assumed a continuous 500 m wall as **superseded** until reconfirmed.
3. Rebuild quantities for the module that is actually funded.
4. Offer clients a **minimum viable documentation** pack: site plan, primary sections, one typical façade bay, and a stone/metal prototype sheet.
5. Keep [D3DDD services](/services/) style discipline — clear titles, measured scopes, no phone-number spam in the title block — so packages stay professional when programmes shrink.

Ambition is allowed. Unversioned ambition is expensive.
