Marble and stone packages in the UAE fail more often on **paper** than on the saw. A villa vanity in Jumeirah, a hotel lobby in Abu Dhabi, or a retail feature wall in Sharjah all share the same risk: the fabricator cuts from an incomplete or outdated drawing while the site is still moving.

This checklist is for contractors, stone yards and project managers who need [marble shop drawings](/marble.html) that a CNC operator and a site supervisor can both trust — without inventing miracle lead times or “AI that replaces the draughtsman.”

## Why Gulf stone packages need stricter drawings

Fit-out programmes in Dubai and Abu Dhabi compress often. Design intent PDFs arrive late; MEP ceilings drop; doors move 40 mm after the stone order is placed. If your only control is a marked-up elevation in WhatsApp, you will pay for it in scrap and remakes.

A usable shop-drawing set answers four questions before anyone loads a slab:

1. **What is each piece?** Unique IDs that match plan, elevation, section and cutting list.
2. **How does it meet its neighbour?** Joint type, open joint width, mitre vs butt, sealant expectation.
3. **What is the edge?** Profile, polish grade, arris, drip, and where the edge is *not* finished (hidden returns).
4. **What is frozen?** Survey or approved dimensions vs “TBC — do not cut.”

If any of those four are fuzzy, the set is not fabrication-ready.

## Checklist before you issue “for fabrication”

### 1. Survey and datum
- Confirm floor levels, wall plumb and opening sizes against site measure — not only against the architect’s IFC set.
- Note tolerances the fabricator can absorb (typical stone joint and lippage expectations) vs what must be shimmed on site.
- Mark **hold points**: anything still awaiting MEP clearance stays “not for cut.”

### 2. Piece schedule
- One row per piece: ID, material / finish, nominal size, edge codes, quantity, location (room + elevation).
- Same ID on plan callouts, elevation tags and the nesting / cutting list.
- Separate **field-cut** allowances from factory-finished pieces so the yard does not polish an edge that will be trimmed on site.

### 3. Joint and layout drawings
- Show book-matching or vein direction where it matters visually (feature walls, island tops, reception desks).
- Dimension open joints and movement joints; do not leave “match existing” as the only instruction.
- Call out substrate, adhesive / mechanical fixing zones, and where drawings stop (e.g. waterproofing by others).

### 4. Edge and detail sheets
- Standard edge library with codes (A, B, C…) referenced from the schedule — not a new freehand profile on every sheet.
- Sections through sinks, hob cut-outs, sockets and upstands with clear clearances.
- Splashback returns and mitred corners drawn, not assumed.

### 5. Revision discipline
- Title block with revision letter, date and a short human-readable change note (“Rev C: island length −30 mm per site measure 12 Oct”).
- Cloud only what changed; archive superseded PDFs so site is not cutting from Rev A in a chat thread.

## What “good enough” looks like on a typical villa kitchen

A Dubai villa kitchen package that travels cleanly usually includes:

- Plan with piece tags and sink / hob centres
- Elevations for each run with heights and upstands
- One section sheet for typical edges and apron details
- A schedule that exports cleanly to Excel for the yard
- A nested cutting layout once slab sizes are known (see our related notes on slab nesting when you publish that workflow)

That is ordinary [AutoCAD drafting](/services/architectural-drafting/) done carefully — not a marketing render. Renders still help clients approve vein direction; they do not replace the fabrication set.

## Common failures we see (and how drawings prevent them)

| Failure on site | Drawing fix |
| --- | --- |
| Island overhang wrong after cabinet change | Hold cut until cabinet IFC + site measure; note “do not cut” on island pieces |
| Vein mismatch across a feature wall | Book-match diagram + slab photo references tied to piece IDs |
| Polished edge where a mitre hides it | Edge codes per face; unfinished faces marked |
| Remakes after MEP moved a socket | Cut-out schedule linked to latest electrical layout revision |
